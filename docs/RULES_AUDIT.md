# Independent rules audit

Reviewed on **October 24, 2026**. This is an evidence log, not a certification that Local Rift implements every legal Riftbound interaction. The executable catalog still covers a subset of the full legal card pool.

The audit started from commit `167ad7e` and continued against the shared working tree. The findings below were reproduced through public `Game` actions before being fixed. Small fixtures construct board positions directly; they do not verify deck construction or card conservation.

## Authoritative references

- Riot's [Rules Hub](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/rules-hub/), checked on the review date, links the [October 9, 2026 Core Rules](https://cmsassets.rgpub.io/sanity/files/dsfx7636/news_live/5c7946f7c3b8c6e0faff85ab6dadc6d2e721ea94.pdf).

## Regression evidence

[tests/rules_audit.rs](../tests/rules_audit.rs) contains nine public-API tests:

1. First burnout completes the draw before cleanup victory.
2. Tornado's scheduled Disempower waits for responses.
3. Pyke cannot see an enemy death when Pyke leaves simultaneously.
4. Kennen chooses its Flow target before finalization completes.
5. A newer Irelia trigger follows an older pending Vex trigger.
6. A Tornado effect created after this turn's ending window does not expire on the next turn instead.
7. Burnout invalidates Kennen's chosen trash object even if an identical card later occupies the same index.
8. Abandon still Predicts after its counter target disappears.
9. Switcheroo exchanges actual negative Might correctly; a later +7 increase only erases a −7 deficit.

The original seven passed after their fixes. The library suite passed **156 tests**, and the library passed Clippy with warnings denied at the end of that batch. The two later failures were separately reproduced before correction; all nine audit tests subsequently passed. These checks establish the named examples, not universal rules equivalence. Full-match simulation, rendering, and API transport checks are maintained separately.

### Staged battlefield follow-up

[tests/staged_battlefields.rs](../tests/staged_battlefields.rs) adds six public-action regressions, built from the original bundled Discipline, Rengar, and Flash rather than synthetic new card effects:

1. A reacting Rengar's controller receives attacker status and initial focus.
2. The turn player chooses either of two staged combats; rejecting an unavailable choice leaves the state unchanged, and the remaining combat opens afterward.
3. A Flash-created non-combat showdown precedes another staged combat; absent control and its Hidden slot are cleared first.
4. Entering during a closed chain only stages combat; it cannot prematurely activate combat designations in an ongoing showdown.
5. Withdrawing a contender cancels its staged combat.
6. The turn player also chooses among multiple non-combat showdowns, with the remaining one preserved.

All six passed after the fixes, alongside the 156-test library suite, 17 expansion-card tests, ten earlier conformance tests, nine payment tests, and seven earlier audit tests. A Hidden-payment fixture was corrected to pass the actual contender's initial focus before testing the other player's Hidden card; the Hidden runtime was unchanged.

### Hold-task follow-up

[tests/holding.rs](../tests/holding.rs) adds four public-action regressions: two Holds from seven points finish at nine; two Holds from six finish at eight; nonwinning Holds generate both Master Yi XP triggers only after both fields have scored and finish those triggers before Channel/Draw; winning Holds resolve neither the XP nor Last Rites triggers and do not channel or draw. The seven-point and winning-trigger fixtures failed before correction. All four pass after the narrow fix, alongside the 175-test library suite, including existing conquest and turn-stage checks.

## Payment review

Read `src/payment.rs` and the hand-play, optional-cost, trash-play, Deflect, Hidden, and ability-payment entry points in `src/game.rs`. The exact-payment wrapper validates on a disposable clone and commits only after all selected sources are consumed. Restrictive card-domain obligations precede flexible Deflect obligations. Spell-only power is excluded from non-spell payments. No additional atomicity or matching-domain defect was confirmed in this review; [tests/payments.rs](../tests/payments.rs) exercises the concrete mixed-source cases.

This review also identified a narrower rune-choice gap: domain-only recycling always preferred exhausted runes. That gap was subsequently closed by `Action::RecycleRune { domain, exhausted }`, which can recycle a ready rune without implicitly exhausting it for energy, while retaining an exhausted rune of the same domain. The implementation and its successful/rejected-action regressions were independently reviewed. All **nine payment tests** and **ten earlier conformance tests** passed after this addition. Automatic payment remains a convenience, while explicit source selection and rune preparation provide the corresponding player choices.

The previously confirmed multiple-Flow-cost selector gap is addressed by `FlowSource`/`FlowOption`. Independent inspection verified explicit printed-versus-granted selection, retained normal timing, refusal to override instructed Fizz/Last Rites plays with Flow, and restricted optional costs preceding flexible printed-Flow costs. All five tests in [tests/flow_costs.rs](../tests/flow_costs.rs) passed on independent rerun, including selected-cost quotes and exact payment, invalid-choice atomicity, expiration, and the lack of extra Reaction permission (829.1.b.2, 829.1.c.3). This is not a claim about unimplemented future non-resource Flow costs.

The first twelve expanded cards were reviewed against their supplied official text, operation mappings, targeting, and regression tests. Their interactions prompted RA-010 above. Riot's [Vendetta FAQ](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/rules-and-releases/vendetta-rules-faq-and-clarifications/) also confirms that Swap calculates separate Might-increasing and Might-decreasing effects; the core rules specify the treatment of negative values.

### Second expansion follow-up

The 22-card expansion uses explicit reviewed metadata for Assault, Tank, Backline, Deflect, Ganking, Ambush, and Accelerate. `Game::unit_keywords` combines printed abilities with token definitions, equipment, Yi's level, and Kennen's granted Assault. Token units do not inherit their creating card's keywords. Shield remained outside the second batch. The third batch adds it explicitly as a separate defender-Might ability under rule 814; it is not inferred from related keywords.

[tests/keyword_expansion.rs](../tests/keyword_expansion.rs) checks the eleven keyword units and four conditional Legion play effects, including repeated Deflect payments, combat-only Assault, Tank/Backline allocation, field-to-field Ganking, conditional Ambush timing, Accelerate payment and atomic rejection, and Legion after a countered earlier card. Dangerous Duo chooses after entering and may choose itself; Trifarian Gloryseeker receives only one persistent buff. Vanguard Captain references its live location when the effect executes. If it leaves the board, it has no location and cannot play the tokens “here” (359.3.e.12, 359.3.f.2); there is no blanket last-known-location fallback.

Matriarch can now choose a cheap Accelerate unit in the trash during trigger finalization and expose its optional play cost when the ability resolves. The chosen object is preserved; the third batch moves it through `PendingPlay` and permits cancellation only when actual finalization is impossible. Its printed base cost is waived, while Accelerate remains payable. Ezreal's optional-cost discount allocation can be changed during this payment choice. These cases are exercised with Legion Rearguard and distinguish the two choices from each other (204, 355, 420.3, 805).

The seven spell mappings and [tests/spell_expansion.rs](../tests/spell_expansion.rs) were independently read. Lunar Boon's continuation retains the physical spell on the chain while its discard/draw instruction executes, preventing burnout from drawing the still-resolving spell. Scrapyard Champion uses the same continuation for its play ability. Fight damage originates from the units and bypasses Void Gate's spell/ability bonus. Recruit placement choices occur during each pending token's finalization, after the parent finishes; one token enters after each choice, with no discretionary response between pending finalizations. All six reviewed token creators use this pending-play path, including fixed-base Birds, Mechs, and exhausted Gold. The initial RA-011 interpretation was corrected after an independent reread of rule 354.3; its regression now checks parent completion, incremental entry, and pending-item age. A companion regression preserves simultaneous ordering for two Vex observers of the same token.


### Third expansion and instructed real-card plays

The 49-card batch (103–151) adds explicit Shield, intrinsic ready entry, and per-finalized-card cost metadata; eight equipment cards contribute their reviewed passive grants through the same live keyword query. Shield adds Might only to an actual defender. It changes lethal damage requirements but does not change Tank/Backline assignment priority. Intrinsic ready entry is not a Ready action. Battering Ram counts cards finalized earlier this turn, including countered spells, and retains its printed one-energy floor.

The on-play target helper covers the new single-target units after they enter, so effects saying “a unit” can choose their own source. Enemy and battlefield restrictions remain explicit. Here-token creators reference the live source when their instruction executes; if that source has left, no token is played. New Birds, Recruits, Mechs, and exhausted Gold reuse the pending token model rather than immediately entering during the parent effect. [tests/third_unit_expansion.rs](../tests/third_unit_expansion.rs) covers these paths, including actual Gust/Flash responses to hidden Fae and Gust responding to Tornado.

[tests/nested_plays.rs](../tests/nested_plays.rs) covers the actual-card counterpart. Public trash targets are selected before response priority, while child choices and costs wait until pending finalization. The new `PendingCard` observation is public and counted as a physical card outside the trash/board/finalized chain. Fizz still waives only printed energy and schedules recycling after a successful finalized play; Matriarch waives printed energy and power but not Accelerate. Last Rites pays the selected unit's costs. An impossible play can be cancelled under 358.5, restoring the original trash object and its granted Flow. Cancellation is not offered merely because the automatic preferred target is unpayable. Riot's older [Origins FAQ](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/rules-and-releases/riftbound-origins-faq/) illustrates public-trash targeting; the current authority for this correction is October 9 Core Rules 355.5 and 355.10.a/.a.1.

The third-batch spell resolver was independently inspected: Repeat executes both operations before the physical spell reaches its destination zone and before cleanup. Existing Bellows death-replacement regressions still apply; Draw Repeat cannot shuffle its own still-resolving card during burnout. Focused passing examples are recorded in the test files; they do not establish full-pool or all-combination parity.

### Fourth expansion: event triggers and adjacent spells

The 64 event-trigger cards (152–215) use explicit descriptors for the event, trigger-time conditions, chosen targets, and operations. Runtime source context distinguishes a unit, an equipment bearer, and standalone gear; a granted ability follows its bearer after the equipment detaches. Source-relative location uses the live object at execution, including the absence of a location after that object leaves. Death events capture the dying source's own relevant facts, while another unit's death observer must survive the simultaneous event (383.2.c.2).

Targets finalize before responses. Beast Below chooses both its friendly and enemy targets in their printed order; Windsinger can decline its optional trigger with no target. Deflect can be generated and paid during trigger finalization, and rejected selections leave state unchanged. Implicit subjects are not target choices. Hidden play effects preserve their original battlefield restrictions and recheck source existence. Conditional Shen Hold triggers check their condition when triggered, rather than again on execution (383.2.a.1). First-time restrictions are per source and turn; simultaneous qualifying deaths do not create multiple Wraith of Echoes triggers (383.1.b).

The review distinguished the acting player from the affected unit's owner. Enemy instructions can make Fretful Feline become ready, but cannot satisfy Irelia's “when you ready me” or Simian Ancestor's “when you buff me” for that unit's controller (412.4, 427.1.c). Intrinsic ready entry is not a Ready action. Completed-play and Draw counts are separate from the earlier card-finalization count used by Legion: countered spells produce no completed-play event, and burning produces no Draw event. Attack and Defend observers fire on the first designation in each combat, including newly entering participants, rather than on every cleanup (383.4.e–f).

[tests/event_triggers.rs](../tests/event_triggers.rs) contains **30 passing public-action tests**, with at least one positive path for each of the 64 descriptors and additional cases for source departure, current “here,” optional and multiple targets, Deflect, equipment-granted abilities, simultaneous deaths, counters, per-turn counts, and movement staging. A focused library test checks an opponent's Buff actor separately. The final focused verification also passed **181 library tests**, all **four Hold-task** and **six staged-battlefield** regressions, and Clippy with warnings denied. These results cover the named examples; they do not enumerate every combination of the expanded catalog.

The 14 adjacent spell mappings (216–229) received an independent source-and-executor review, with 17 focused scalar-spell tests maintained separately. The review checked that Gentlemen's Duel requires all mandatory targets before finalization (355.8), Wages of Pain still produces Gold after losing its target, and Gentlemen's Duel can still give the friendly target its independent +3 when the enemy becomes invalid (359.3.e.1/.e.5). Alternative base costs, additional costs, and discounts follow that order (356.1–4); Repeat retains separately chosen targets (820.2). A physical Draw spell remains outside the trash until every repeated instruction finishes, so burnout cannot recycle its own still-resolving card. This is a bounded review of these mappings, not a full rules-equivalence claim.

### Activated gear completion follow-up

The read-only legend-foundation review reproduced RA-018 against published commit `4fb9d6c`; the permanent reproduction is now in [tests/equipment_abilities.rs](../tests/equipment_abilities.rs). ### Independent legend identity and three foundation powers

The reviewed legend registry now separates a legend from its preset index and chosen champion. Existing legend identities 0–3 remain stable; Hand of Noxus, Herald of the Arcane, and Lady of Luminosity, Starter occupy 4–6. A custom Viktor list may choose Leader instead of Innovator without changing its legend. Canonical chain-source metadata distinguishes a legend ability from a main-deck card ability even when their legacy artwork/reference card IDs coincide. This does not register the unimplemented legends from the larger imported inventory.

Darius's exhaust cost and Reaction timing precede the Legion clause. Legion becomes active when another card has finalized that turn, including a subsequently countered spell; playing a token does not satisfy it (185, 420.4.b, 812.1.b–c). The implementation permits exhausting before Legion is active and adds zero energy. It treats that activation as immediate Add, preserving priority and a pending payment choice. This is a **rule-derived reading**, not a located Hand of Noxus-specific FAQ: 377.2.b restricts explicit use conditions, 727.1.b.1 retains inactive dependent text for evaluation, 721.2 suppresses the inactive instruction, and 401.2 classifies an ability containing Add for immediate resolution. Riot's [Vendetta FAQ](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/rules-and-releases/vendetta-rules-faq-and-clarifications/) independently illustrates evaluation of inactive text in its Heimerdinger/Jayce ruling. Rules 430.2–3 preserve immediate resolution and payment-window activation. Automatic opponents avoid spending this activation when it would do nothing.

Viktor pays one energy and exhausts during finalization, only in his controller's neutral open Main state (310.1.a, 381.1). His ability waits for responses. On resolution, its Recruit enters the existing pending-token process: the parent leaves the finalized chain before the token's location choice and entry (185.2.a, 337.1, 354.3, 355.2). The token can enter the base or a controlled battlefield and generates normal unit-play observers without becoming a played card. Lux observes completed spell plays, compares printed energy cost, and creates a separate respondable draw trigger (131.2, 356.1.c, 420.4.a). Countered spells do not trigger Lux; simultaneous legend and unit observers remain an orderable event batch. Inactive/active timing was independently reviewed against the current Core Rules; older FAQ examples are supporting context rather than authority over the October rules.

[tests/legend_foundation.rs](../tests/legend_foundation.rs) supplies 15 public-action examples covering these three paths, including the actual Back to Back → Hard Bargain → Darius payment interaction, opponent-turn reactions, rejected timing/targets/costs, resource expiry, token observers, and simultaneous Lux sources. The low-cost Repeat example verifies that a repeated spell below Lux's printed threshold does not qualify; it does not claim coverage of a qualifying Repeat spell, which is absent from this reviewed pool. The Strategic opponent also has two new tests for all seven public legend identities and invariance to an opponent's private saved list. Its hypothetical card pool follows complete public deck eligibility, including Signature restrictions, rather than guessing identity from the chosen champion or sampling the private recipe.

### Unit and gear activation foundation

The ten reviewed cards at IDs 230–239 use explicit activation descriptors rather than reading ability costs or timing from flattened card keywords. Each activation records its live unit/gear source, definition, and stable ability index. Renata's two abilities remain separate choices. Ordinary abilities require their controller's neutral open Main state; Dragonsoul Sage's Reaction Add resolves immediately and preserves priority and pending payment (377, 381.1, 430.2–3). A Reaction on an activated-ability clause does not grant Reaction to the source card. The speed parser excludes those colon-delimited clauses, with regression coverage preserving actual Reaction and Quick-Draw card play. Attached equipment's printed rules are inactive, and tokens do not inherit abilities from the card used to describe their artwork (185, 718, 721).

Costs and targets finalize before responses. Matching power and each Deflect obligation use the existing exact-payment path; spell-only power cannot fund these non-spell abilities. Battlefield restrictions on Xerath, Caitlyn, and Renata govern activation. A legal source subsequently moving away does not negate an independent instruction. Caitlyn instead reads her live Might during execution; an absent Caitlyn has no last-known Might, and a target recalled to base is no longer legal (359.3.e.2/.e.12). Arena Bar rechecks whether its target remains exhausted, including when Irelia's actual choose trigger readies it first. Orb's minimum is applied when its modifier is created, not as a permanent floor or a positive correction to an already lower value (479.4.b/.e).

Gear completion uses the corrected RA-018 event: ineffective resolutions still complete, while Not So Fast countering Iron Ballista produces no use event and refunds no paid Exhaust cost. Vanguard Armory queues three pending Recruits; the parent finishes before their individual location choices and entries (354.3, 355.2). Renata's point instruction gains a point directly; it is not battlefield Scoring, Conquering, or Holding, and can grant the winning point without the final-conquest restriction (470.2, 473.1.a.1).

[tests/activated_abilities.rs](../tests/activated_abilities.rs) contains **23 passing tests** covering all ten sources and both Renata indices, rejected costs/timing/targets, source and target changes, exact Deflect payments, immediate resource continuation, pending token placement, mandatory-child feasibility, actual ability counters, AI winning-point and lethal-damage choices, and the independent Tank/Backline priorities. The additional payment and combat failures are recorded as RA-019 and RA-020 above. Existing equipment, nested-play, keyword, payment, conformance, and all **189 library tests** also pass; the focused library and activation test targets pass Clippy with warnings denied. These examples do not implement the next object-cost, kill/discard-cost, or energy/power-conversion activations; future conversion feasibility requires obligation-aware search rather than blindly using every resource source.

## Open questions and next audit targets

These items are **not resolved by the passing examples**:

- **Separate events within one resolving item:** RA-011 models pending token plays and their later entry events, while RA-005 preserves older pending groups during finalization. RA-012 extends the pending model to the included Fizz, Last Rites, and Matriarch instructions. A broader audit must still distinguish other separate instructions and future nested play effects; a single cleanup is not itself proof that all resulting triggers were simultaneous.
- **Expanded card pool:** new keywords, replacement interactions, granted abilities, zone changes, and cards not in the original presets require dedicated executable behavior and tests. Importing their official text is not implementation coverage.

The audit read the original 69 card texts and the execution paths for targeting, pending triggers, death replacement, scoring, turn start/end, movement, combat, and resource payment. It did not exhaustively enumerate every pair or longer combination of those cards, and it did not audit the full currently legal card pool.
